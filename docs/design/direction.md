## What the winners share, and what my first build got wrong

My second build was a dark page with an ember glow, a serif headline and soft cards. The owner rejected it as generic, and the research shows why. The six award-winning sites studied above are mostly **light, ruled and typographic**. They build pages from **hairline cells**, not floating cards. They use **huge tight uppercase grotesk**, often cut with an italic serif. They label everything in **mono caps**. They give **one sharp accent** a single job. Their texture comes from **data, drawing marks or dithering**, not glows.

## The direction

**The case drawing: every screen is an engineering plate, a ruled sheet of cells on warm paper with a title block, registration marks and section hatching. One orange square is kept for a move that fires.**

Why it fits the product sentence:
- *"A memory"*: an engineering drawing is a durable, numbered record. Each screen is a **plate** (00–15). Each has a **title block** naming its data kind, source files and build commit. That is the product's honesty rule written as a drawing convention.
- *"Notices … slowly"*: time runs across every plate. The ten xz quarters are a row of cells, each stamped HELD. Each cell carries a field of tiny squares, **one per message** in Pressure's 120-day window: filled if read, hollow if not. The data supplies the texture. It also shows the plainest truth in the files: in every quarter, 0 of those messages were read.
- *"A calm question"*: no alarm colours, no glow. The accent appears as a small orange square, like an annotation point on a drawing. It marks the figures that matter and is reserved, as a status, for a fired move. No move fired on xz, so no status square is orange.
- *Honesty*: a NOT COMPUTED value is drawn with **section hatching**, the 45° lines a draughtsman uses for a cut. Its reason sits on a paper label laid over the hatching. The blank is a designed object, not an apology.
- *Copy*: heavy uppercase statements that the data backs ("10 QUARTERS. 10 HOLDS."), then a turn in italic serif ("*Not one all-clear.*"). The structure is borrowed from Neutomni's mixed sentence and Moto's staccato lines.

## Directions considered and rejected

1. **Dark instrument with an ember glow** (my second build). Rejected by the owner, and rightly. A glow is the most common move in AI-generated pages. Dark-plus-accent is the default for "security", and soft cards hide structure.
2. **Paper ledger with a serif and a red pencil** (my first build). It read as a document, not a product, and had no grid and no presence.
3. **Security console: neon on black, threat meters.** It sells fear. That contradicts *a calm question*, and it would make an honest NOT COMPUTED look like a failure.
4. **Playful illustration** (Unseen, Family). It trivialises a story that involves a real person who was targeted.
